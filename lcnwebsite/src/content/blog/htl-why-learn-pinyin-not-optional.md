often i see Chinese learners ask about if they should learn Chinese Pinyin 拼音 on forums like Reddit like [this post]() and there is some confusing in the Chinese learning community. For some reason Pinyin is down played in it's importance or seen as a secondary assistance tool, with Hanzi and radicals being the main area of focus and fascination. There are even entire apps dedicated to Hanzi alone. Do not get me wrong, i find Hanzi characters fascinating as much as anyone else for it's rich history and cultural heritage, but I want to explain why having a strong foundation of pinyin is not optional if you want to become literate in Chinese as a non-native 2nd language learner. being literate is the goal right? if it is not for you, then you can stop reading here.

Pinyin is the foundation of the Mandarin pronounciation system. school children in China learn pinyin so they can pronounce and better remember Chinese Hanzi characters. if Pinyin is part of the standard Chinese education, why would it make sense to think that you can become proficient and literate in Chinese without these foundations? the fact of the matter is, China has more than 50 regional dialects with vastly different pronounciations of words. As an example, my mother dialect is Fuzhouhua, and I learned English as my first formal language in the US, and it was only after my 20s that I started studying Mandarin Chinese. In China, Pinyin is used as the tool to make sure that all kids can speak standard Mandarin no matter where they are from or what their family speaks. Pinyin also increased the Chinese literacy rate from something like low teens in the 1900s to over 90% now. Seriously, ask any native Chinese speaker how they learned Chinese in grade school and they will tell you that they started with Pinyin. As a non-native learner of Chinese, if you do not have a strong foundation of Pinyin, nobody will know what you are saying which brings me to the next point.

Mandarin pronounciate is hard, really hard. there is no other way to say this. My Fuzhouhua roots probably help with hearing tones because Fuzhouhua has 7 tones vs. 4/5 in Mandarin, but the tunes are quite different.

with the added complexity of tones, there are around 1,300 unique sounds that you can make to represent a concept/character. English has far more unique sounds, but they are distinct and can be easier to separate apart. the 汉字 characters themselves have phonetic elements for example 莫 `mo4`，摸 `mo1`，磨 `mo2` all have the right component that hints at the `mo` sound, but it is far easier to remember the characters if you know all the pinyin sounds well and can link it back to the particular mouth positions that produce the sounds for each character. also many sounds are similar like `shan1` and `shang1` so you need to know the difference.

i see many so "Chinese experts" avocating methods that do not include Pinyin at all and focus purely on creating symbolic memetics for each Chinese character without any notice of the pinyin sounds or sentence context in which the character is used. Ok sure, you might feel super productive and remember 500 characters in record time in an abstract way that links those Hanzi symbols to other made symbols that then link to English words in your mind. however, you are no closer to literacy, which in my humble opinion is the goal for learning Chinese because literacy is what unlocks that world of culture and knowledge that you can not access with just translated works.

the 3rd and practical reason for knowing pinyin is that in the modern world, to be productice, study and work in China, you have to use smartphones and computers. every modern computer system uses some form of pinyin input with the 26 character input being the most common. you will not see people in the 20s and 30s writing Chinese characters with thier thumb because writing each stroke is 20-30x slower than typing "cflmy" which gets autocompleted to “吃饭了没有？”, you will only see older people in the 50s and 60s who still write with thier hands and you know this because it takes 20x longer for them to respond. infact, there is even apps that teach older Chinese pinyin in China so they can be more efficient with tech.

the final and most important reason for literacy is that Pinyin helps you remember more words because it acts as an anchor system. when learning new words, i often see learners complain that they forget it even after spending lots of time writing each stroke. often the reason is because there is nothing for the new concept to latch on to, there is no anchor. this is the reason that "experts" invented elaborate menomic systemms for remember Hanzi components. but none of that is actually required because the perfect anchor system are the pinyin and the surrounding sentence itself. this is why you never want to learn characters and even words in isolation. the single unit of learning should be the sentence, not the Hanzi or radical.

for example, does not the word 乒乓 sound like pingpong itself, and what about 咖啡 kafei and 键盘 jianpan? the sounds create a image in your mind so you need less memetic techniques that waste storage space in your head.

people often argue that knowing the Pinyin does not reveal anything about the meaning or structure of the sentence, because Chinese characters often have the same pronunciation and tones. while this is correct and sounds smart in theory, it is not the case in practice. i would say that 90% of sentences can be deduced from just the pinyin alone (even without the tone mark) such as "ninmen de gongsi zuijin fazhan haoma?" can only mean "您们的公司最近发展好吗？", you can try this yourself, not ask an AI to deduce what the plain pinyin means.

pinyin is the bridge that leads to geniune memorisation of the characters, which reading full sentences, and eventual literacy. it also help you be productive and use modern tools and services whether you are chating on Wechat, filling out a digital form, or looking up words in Pleco. literacy will help you consume Chinese native content and get access to the vast amounts of knowledge that was and will never be translated.

if the goal is litaracy, being productice, and not short term dopamine hits from remembering lots of isolated Chinese characters with abstract memetic techniques, then i have to break it to you, learning pinyin is not optional and should be prioritize.
