作者在 AI 行业工作。有一次他回<mark class="vocab">老家</mark>，和高中同学在烧烤店吃饭。有人问：新闻里天天说<mark class="vocab">大模型</mark>、人工智能，这到底是什么？能不能帮我们干活？

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>The author works in the AI industry. On a trip back to his hometown, he was eating barbecue with high-school classmates when someone asked: the news talks about large models and artificial intelligence every day—what is that, exactly? Can it help us get work done?</p>
</details>

他马上有了兴趣。自己每天都在和 AI 打交道：测试模型、搭建智能体、写工作流。他的信息流里，十句话有八句都和 AI 有关。朋友圈里也天天有人讨论新框架、新模型、新跑分。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>He immediately perked up. His own work is dealing with AI every day: testing models, building agents, writing workflows. In his information feed, eight out of ten posts are about AI. In WeChat Moments, people discuss new frameworks, new models, and new benchmark scores every day.</p>
</details>

可是一桌子同学的回答让他很意外。有人用豆包问过两次菜谱；有人让 DeepSeek 写请假条，写得很差，还不如自己写；当老师的同学偶尔用它查资料，但不敢全信，怕它「<mark class="vocab">瞎编</mark>」。剩下几个人几乎没用过。不是买不起，也不是不会下载 App，只是觉得跟自己没关系。有个同学说了一句让他到现在都记得的话：AI 不就是<mark class="vocab">聊天机器人</mark>吗，跟以前的<mark class="vocab">智能客服</mark>有什么区别？

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>But the answers around the table surprised him. One person had used Doubao twice to ask for recipes. Another had asked DeepSeek to write a leave request; it came out badly, worse than writing it himself. A classmate who is a teacher sometimes uses it to look things up, but does not fully trust it, afraid it will "make things up." The rest had barely used AI at all. It was not that they could not afford it, or did not know how to download an app; they just felt it had nothing to do with them. One classmate said something the author still remembers: isn't AI just a chatbot? How is it different from the old intelligent customer-service bots?</p>
</details>

作者心里很震惊。不是因为他们说错了。说实话，他们说得挺对。对大多数人来说，AI 现在确实就是一个更聪明一点的<mark class="vocab">搜索框</mark>。可是这和他在行业里的感觉完全不是一个世界。他突然明白：自己可能活在一个很大的<mark class="vocab">信息茧房</mark>里。这不是算法推荐的茧房，而是<mark class="vocab">圈子</mark>自带的茧房。身边的人都在聊 AI，他就以为全世界都在聊。出了这个<mark class="vocab">圈子</mark>，很多人只是偶尔打开豆包问一个问题。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>He was shocked—not because they were wrong. Honestly, they were quite right. For most people, AI really is just a slightly smarter search box. But that feeling is a completely different world from the one he lives in at work. He suddenly realized he may be living inside a large information cocoon. It is not the kind created by algorithmic recommendations; it is the cocoon that comes with the in-group. Everyone around him talks about AI, so he assumed the whole world was talking about it. Outside that circle, many people only open Doubao once in a while to ask a question.</p>
</details>

回来以后，他把 AI 使用者分成六个等级。第一等最多，大概占九成以上：打开 App，问菜谱、法律、作业、旅游，用完就走，不太相信答案。第二等会试试做图、做 PPT，但质量一般，试几次就觉得「也就那样」，然后不再深入。第三等开始有<mark class="vocab">门槛</mark>：会保存<mark class="vocab">提示词</mark>、搭<mark class="vocab">工作流</mark>，身边的人已经叫他们「AI 达人」。可是这些方法很难往下传，因为第一等的人会觉得「问个问题至于吗」。从这一层开始，每往上走一层，能交流的人就少一批。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>After he returned, he roughly divided AI users into six levels. The first level is the largest, perhaps more than 90%: they open an app, ask about recipes, the law, homework, or travel, then leave, without trusting the answers much. The second level tries extra features like making images or PowerPoints, but the quality is average; after a few tries they think "that's about it" and stop going deeper. The third level is where a real barrier appears: they save prompts, build workflows, and people around them already call them "AI experts." But these methods are hard to pass down, because first-level users think, "It's just asking a question—is that really necessary?" From this layer on, each step up means fewer people you can actually talk to.</p>
</details>

第四等开始搭<mark class="vocab">智能体</mark>，想让 AI 自己跑起来，但成果只能讲给同类听。第五等是业余爱好者，会用 AI 写代码、做知识库，很在乎<mark class="vocab">性价比</mark>，宁可多花时间改，也不愿多付二十块钱。第六等最少，可能不到 1%：他们用最好的工具，一个月愿意花几百块，把 AI 当成同事。第一等觉得 AI 是<mark class="vocab">聊天机器人</mark>，第六等觉得 AI 是不知疲倦的同事。这不是程度不同，而是维度不同。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>The fourth level starts building agents, wanting AI to run on its own, but the results can only be shared with people like themselves. The fifth level is amateur enthusiasts: they use AI to write code and build knowledge bases, care a lot about value for money, and would rather spend two extra hours fixing things than pay another twenty yuan. The sixth level is the smallest, perhaps under 1%: they use the best tools, are willing to spend a few hundred yuan a month, and treat AI as a colleague. First-level users think AI is a chatbot; sixth-level users think AI is a tireless coworker. This is not a difference of degree. It is a difference of dimension.</p>
</details>

作者还发现，对前面几等人来说，一个能在自己电脑上运行的小模型，大概就够用了。写报告、整理资料、做简单计划，并不需要最强的<mark class="vocab">大模型</mark>。企业也一样，很多公司更需要「数据不出门、成本可控」的<mark class="vocab">本地模型</mark>。所以行业每天在追<mark class="vocab">参数</mark>、<mark class="vocab">推理能力</mark>和更长的<mark class="vocab">上下文</mark>，真正受益的是第六等人；第一等人从旧模型问到新模型，体感差不多，反正都是在问菜谱。技术进步当然好，但收益很不均匀。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>He also found that for the first few levels, a small model that can run on their own computer is probably enough. Writing reports, organizing materials, and making simple plans do not require the strongest large models. Companies are similar: many firms mainly need a local model whose data stays inside and whose cost is under control. So while the industry chases parameters, reasoning ability, and longer context every day, the real beneficiaries are sixth-level users. For first-level users, going from an old model to a new one feels almost the same—they are still just asking for recipes. Technical progress is of course good, but the gains are very uneven.</p>
</details>

回<mark class="vocab">老家</mark>时，朋友们的生活并没有因为 AI 发生本质变化：该上班上班，该带孩子带孩子。没有人因为不会用 AI 而失业，也没有人因为会用 AI 而升职。AI 对普通人的影响，更多是情绪上的：新闻说「AI 又取代某个岗位」，大家会<mark class="vocab">焦虑</mark>；真去用，又觉得没那么神。<mark class="vocab">从业者</mark>正好相反。他们确实靠 AI 提高效率，但必须一直学习，一周不学就觉得落后。最<mark class="vocab">讽刺</mark>的是：完全不用 AI 的人最不<mark class="vocab">焦虑</mark>，最懂 AI 的人反而最<mark class="vocab">焦虑</mark>。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>When he went home, his friends' lives had not really changed because of AI: they still went to work and still took care of their kids. Nobody had lost a job for not using AI, and nobody had been promoted for using it well. For ordinary people, AI's impact is more emotional: news stories about "AI replacing another job" make them anxious, but when they actually try it, it does not feel that magical. Industry people are the opposite. They do use AI to work more efficiently, but they also have to keep learning; if they skip a week, they feel behind. The most ironic part is this: the people who do not use AI at all are the least anxious, while the people who understand AI best are the most anxious.</p>
</details>

最后，作者用 2005 年的网卡作比喻。那时候「上网」是一件要专门做的事：装网卡、拉网线、去网吧。后来有了无线网络和手机，互联网变成空气，人们不再觉得自己在「使用互联网技术」，只是在生活。AI 现在还处在「要专门打开 App 去用」的阶段。真正成熟以后，它应该藏在短视频推荐、医院检查、公司报表后面，变成看不见的<mark class="vocab">基础设施</mark>。到那时，<mark class="vocab">老家</mark>的朋友也不会再说「AI 只是<mark class="vocab">聊天机器人</mark>」——不是因为他们学会了，而是因为他们已经<mark class="vocab">不知不觉</mark>地在用。在那一天到来之前，<mark class="vocab">老家</mark>朋友的判断可能更准确：AI 现在确实还是<mark class="vocab">聊天机器人</mark>，只是<mark class="vocab">从业者</mark>不愿意承认。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>Finally, the author uses a 2005 network card as a metaphor. Back then, "going online" was something you had to do on purpose: install a network card, run a cable, go to an internet cafe. Later came Wi-Fi and phones, and the internet became like air. People no longer felt they were "using internet technology"; they were just living. AI is still in the stage where you have to open an app on purpose to use it. When it is truly mature, it should hide behind short-video recommendations, hospital exams, and company reports, becoming invisible infrastructure. At that point, his hometown friends will no longer say "AI is just a chatbot"—not because they learned how to use it, but because they are already using it without noticing. Until that day comes, his friends' judgment may be more accurate: AI really is still a chatbot. People in the industry just do not want to admit it.</p>
</details>

一句话概括：AI 圈里的人每天都在追新模型，但大多数普通人只用它问问题；技术进步主要帮了少数人，普通人得到的更多是<mark class="vocab">焦虑</mark>。AI 真正成熟，是它变成空气，而不是变成一个更强的聊天窗口。

<details class="translation">
<summary>English translation</summary>
<p>In one sentence: people in the AI circle chase new models every day, but most ordinary people only use AI to ask questions; technical progress mainly helps a small group, while ordinary people mostly get anxiety. AI will be truly mature when it becomes like air, not when it becomes a stronger chat window.</p>
</details>
